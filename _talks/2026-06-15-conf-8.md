## Extra information of our framework:

### Likelihoods of atmospheric protection

Based on the topology of a dipole field, the fractional area of the planetary atmosphere 
exposed to the stellar wind is defined by the unprotected polar cap angle 
$$\alpha_0 = \arcsin \left( (r_\mathrm{M}/r_\mathrm{pl})^{-1/2} \right)$$ [(Vidotto et al. 2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A%26A...557A..67V/abstract),
where $$r_\mathrm{M}$$ is the magnetopause standoff distance and $$r_\mathrm{pl}$$ is the planetary radius.   
Following [Rodríguez-Mozos & Moya (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A%26A...630A..52R/abstract),
we define an atmospheric protection likelihood $$P(\alpha_0)$$ based on
a Gaussian decay of this unprotected angle

$$
P(\alpha_0) = \left\{ \begin{array}{lcc} 1 & \text{if} & \alpha_0 \leq \alpha_\mathrm{0,crit} \\ \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}  \left[ \frac{\alpha_0 - \alpha_\mathrm{0,crit}}{\sigma} \right]^2 \right)  & \text{if} & \alpha_0 > \alpha_\mathrm{0,crit} \end{array} \right. .
$$

where $$\alpha_\mathrm{0,crit} \approx 26.6^\circ$$ corresponds to a standoff distance of $$r_\mathrm{M}/r_\mathrm{pl} = 5$$,
a regime consistent with Paleoarchean Earth [(Tarduno et al. 2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010Sci...327.1238T/abstract).
The decay parameter $$\sigma$$ is calibrated such that the probability is 0 when $$\alpha_0 = 45^\circ$$, which corresponds to the
limit of $$r_\mathrm{M}/r_\mathrm{pl}$$ = 2 proposed by [Lammer et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007AsBio...7..185L/abstract).


### Stellar wind environment

The total stellar pressure is given by

$$
p_\star(r_\mathrm{orb}) = \frac{B_\star^2(r_\mathrm{orb})}{2\mu_0} + 
\frac{\dot{M}_\star}{4\pi v_r r_\mathrm{orb}^2} \left[ v_\mathrm{rel}^2 + \frac{2 k_\mathrm{B}}{m_\mathrm{P}} T(r_\mathrm{orb}) \right]
$$

where

$$
B_\star(r) = \left\{ \begin{array}{lcc} B_0 \left( \frac{R_\star}{r} \right)^3 & \text{if} & r \leq 2.5 R_\star \\ 0.4 B_0 \left( \frac{R_\star}{r}\right)^2  & \text{if} & r > 2.5 R_\star \end{array} \right.  
$$

is the stellar magnetic field,

$$
\dot{M}_\star = \dot{M}_\odot \left( \frac{R_\star}{R_\odot}\right)^{0.46} \left( \frac{R_\mathrm{X} L_\mathrm{bol}}{R_\mathrm{X,\odot} L_\mathrm{bol,\odot}}\right)^{0.77}
$$

is the stellar mass-loss rate, based on the X-ray flux relation reported by [Wood et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...37W/abstract), where

$$
R_\mathrm{X} = \left\{ \begin{array}{lcc} R_\mathrm{X, sat} & \text{if} & \mathrm{Ro} \leq \mathrm{Ro}_\mathrm{sat}^\mathrm{crit} \\ R_\mathrm{X,sat} \left(\mathrm{Ro}/\mathrm{Ro}_\mathrm{sat}^\mathrm{crit}\right)^\beta  & \text{if} & \mathrm{Ro} > \mathrm{Ro}_\mathrm{sat}^\mathrm{crit} \end{array} \right.
$$

with $$R_\mathrm{X,sat}\approx 7.41 \cdot 10^{-4}$$, $$\mathrm{Ro}_\mathrm{sat}^\mathrm{crit} \approx 0.16$$ and $$\beta \approx -2.70$$ [(Wright et al. 2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...743...48W/abstract).    
The radial component of the velocity is given by

$$
v_r(r) = \sqrt{\frac{2 G M_\star}{R_\star}} \left( 1 - \frac{R_\star}{r} \right)
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{v}_\mathrm{rel}(r) = v_r \mathbf{\hat{r}} + (v_\phi - v_\mathrm{orb})\mathbf{\hat{\phi}}
$$

is the velocity of the planet relative to the stellar wind, with $$v_\mathrm{orb} = \sqrt{G M_\star/r_\mathrm{orb}}$$, and

$$
v_\phi(r) = \left\{ \begin{array}{lcc} \Omega_\star r & \text{if} & r \leq R_\mathrm{A} \\ \frac{\Omega_\star R_\mathrm{A}^2}{r}  & \text{if} & r > R_\mathrm{A} \end{array} \right. ,
$$

where $$R_\mathrm{A}$$ is the Alfvén radius. Finally, the temperature profile $$T(r)$$ is given by the models of 
[Johnstone et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A%26A...577A..27J/abstract).
